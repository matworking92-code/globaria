How figures are shown

Local prototype. Safety index scores are Numbeo figures (0–100, higher is safer), from the 2026 contributor survey. Color on the score follows that same index (Very safe, Safe, Moderate, Caution, Concern — higher is safer). Prices are shown in local currency and US dollars (approximate). Tuition figures prefer the university’s published 2026–27 (or latest) rate for a named program; city 1-bed rents use Numbeo city-centre asking rents (August 2026 contributors). Daily-cost marks ($ to $$$$$) use Numbeo’s 2026 Cost of Living Index excluding rent, scaled so the United States = 100: Much cheaper, Cheaper, About the same, More expensive, Much more expensive — groceries, restaurants, transport, and utilities, not rent. 1-bed apartment figures are shown in US dollars (a rough conversion from the local asking rent). Halls are a typical city (or country) figure unless a campus is known to differ — no halls, housing included in tuition, or a clearly different range. Program names are a snapshot sample (up to eight), taken from the school’s English-taught list, not a complete catalogue. Program length is a typical national structure (for example a three-year bachelor’s in Norway or England, a four-year honours degree in Scotland or Ireland) or a named length in the program title. Confirm with the school. A campus is listed when a full English-taught bachelor or master’s exists, an international can apply on a real admissions page, a named-year fee or an honest confirm note is attached, and the school is a recognised degree-granting institution in that country. Listings are demand-weighted for US students: popular degree destinations are deep; other countries may be a 1–2 campus presence listing, not a complete national directory. Index listings name a campus and English-taught programs but carry no tuition — they are excluded from the tuition slider and from country/city tuition medians so unconfirmed fees cannot skew the comparison. QS, THE, and ARWU chips mean the institution appears in that publisher’s global table; they are not a GlobalEd rank or a paid placement. Cost and ranking are not inclusion gates. EU/EEA fee rates apply where the university publishes a separate price; elsewhere the international rate is shown for both.
